# UK running-race search opportunity check

Checked 4 November 2026. The [50-query table](search_queries_50.tsv) records exact queries and the first three URLs returned by the available search service. It samples ten queries each for event names, race-day logistics, cut-offs, elevation and regional/discovery intent. It is a qualitative SERP sample, not a ranking audit or a search-volume dataset. The service result order is **not asserted to be Google organic ranking**; two direct Google searches were spot-checked separately.

| Query group | First result was official organiser | What the sample suggests |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Event name/year | 9/10 | Official race sites are strong for navigational searches. A new directory has a difficult route to clicks. |
| Logistics | 6/10 | Organiser travel, parking and FAQ pages often answer the question directly; dated or unclear guidance creates a limited opportunity for edition-specific summaries. |
| Cut-off | 7/10 | Official rules usually remain the authoritative source. Scope by distance and edition matters more than sheer profile length. |
| Elevation | 2/10 | Specialist route sites and other publishers appear often; a verified numerical ascent with source and route version may be useful, but competition is established. |
| Discovery | 0/10 | Broad regional/terrain searches are contested by directories, articles and event calendars. Curation and genuinely useful filters are required. |

The classification is a narrow host check, and some first results may be generic pages rather than an exact answer. It should not be interpreted as market share, ranking difficulty or click-through rate. Direct Google spot checks found an official Cardiff travel page prominent for a 2027 parking query and a Manchester cut-off query where an AI summary confused registration closing with a race-day cut-off. That ambiguity is a user problem a carefully labelled page could solve, but it is not evidence of easy traffic.

## Search-volume evidence

No credible absolute UK monthly search-volume source was accessible in the pilot. **No monthly search numbers or traffic forecast are claimed.** [Google Trends, GB web search, past 12 months](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=GB&q=half%20marathon,marathon,10k) showed average *relative*, normalised interest of approximately 5 for “half marathon”, 15 for “marathon” and 4 for “10k” in that comparison. A separate [Manchester comparison](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=GB&q=manchester%20marathon,manchester%20marathon%20cut%20off,manchester%20marathon%20elevation,manchester%20marathon%20bag%20drop) averaged about 8 for the race name and 0 for the three modifiers. A third [discovery comparison](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?geo=GB&q=half%20marathon%20near%20me,flat%20half%20marathons,trail%20races,marathon%20cut%20off) averaged about 29, 2, 19 and 12 respectively. These numbers are comparison-specific Trends indices, **not comparable across the three charts**, and zero means insufficient relative data in that comparison, not zero searches.

## Content implications

1. **Prioritise fields that answer a concrete decision:** course-specific cut-off, numerical ascent with route edition, start/finish, transport, parking, baggage/water/toilets, mandatory kit and date of verification. Label unknowns explicitly. A long generic description is unlikely to help.
2. **Use exact edition and distance pages only when verified.** A 2027 landing page that links a 2026 FAQ cannot support a 2027 logistical claim without confirmation. Multiple-distance events need course-level facts.
3. **Offer curated discovery pages when inventory supports them:** for example a region, distance and terrain combination with verified practical filters. Broad generic directory pages face strong incumbents and risk thin content.
4. **Use original presentation and source links.** Do not copy organiser course prose, maps or photos. Mark preliminary or unknown details, and give last-checked dates.
5. **Treat traffic as an experiment after authorised publication.** Google Search Console impressions, query/page pairs, indexing and CTR should decide whether to expand a page format. There is currently no verified basis for traffic, conversion or revenue forecasts.

The main weakness is that the most comprehensive official pages also tend to be the most authoritative search results. Conversely, races with poorer official pages often lack enough trustworthy current facts for a valuable directory profile. The pilot therefore supports selective content validation and measured search testing, not a claim that a 200–300-profile database will automatically earn organic reach.
